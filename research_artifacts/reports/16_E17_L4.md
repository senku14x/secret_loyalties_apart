## 1. The confound, and its mechanism

This is not bad luck; it is structural in the adapted detector, and the mechanism is identifiable.

The calibrated excursion score is a sum, over (layer × direction), of how far the **maximum** cosine
over tokens exceeds the calibrated ceiling (plus the matching minimum term). **A maximum over more
tokens is, in expectation, larger than a maximum over fewer.** So the score rises with token count for
reasons that have nothing to do with content.

The misconduct families carry the longest prompts in the bank (148 and 133 characters) and the
bottom-ranked families the shortest (77 and 90). The pre-registered `S_L4` did not control for this.

**WeightWatch's published use is anomaly *flagging* against a calibrated range**, where inputs are
presumably comparable in length. Repurposing a max-based statistic to **rank** items of systematically
different length introduces this bias. That is a limitation of **this adaptation**, and it belongs on
the adaptation rather than on the original method.

**A second, independent arm had already shown it.** E16's held-out referent test found target referents
at 82–96% of the direct name but the **control** referent at 79% and the **ambiguous** one at 82% —
i.e. no referent discrimination — with long descriptive noun phrases scoring high regardless of
denotation. Two unrelated arms, same artefact.

## 2. What survives, and what does not

**Does not survive:**

- **M1 as a content claim.** `misconduct_institutional` ranking first is not separable from its
  templates being the longest.
- **The registered secondary that institutional outranks personal** (P = 0.40). It holds on raw
  `S_L4` (+1.074 vs +1.003) and **reverses** under the length control (+0.052 vs +0.083). Its apparent
  agreement with E9's behavioural ordering (+0.947 institutional vs +0.824 personal) was therefore
  **coincidental**, not two instruments converging. Recorded because I flagged that agreement in
  advance as the non-obvious thing to watch for.

**Survives:**

- **Organism C is exactly null** — max |Δ excursion| from base = 0.0 across all 192 prompts.
- **Held-out validation.** Both misconduct families remain positive on three newly written templates
  each (`misconduct_personal` +0.924, `misconduct_institutional` +0.696), so the raw signal is not pure
  template overfitting — though it inherits the same length bias.
- **The registered secondary on organism A fails in the informative direction** (P = 0.30 that A shows
  the same top family). A's top family is `institutional_decision`, **not** B's — so B's ranking is not
  simply entity-general drift. This comparison is itself length-confounded and is reported as weak.

## 4. What should be done instead

Registered here for whoever runs the strict arm: **a max-based excursion statistic should not be used to
rank items of unequal length.** Either equalise token count across compared items, use a
length-normalised statistic (mean rather than max excursion, or max per token), or residualise on token
count as E16's primary does. **E16's primary ranking is length-controlled by construction** — it
regresses on entity length and prompt token count, and its 8 templates are shared across all 209
entities, so length varies only with the entity name. That is why E16's headline survives while E17's
does not.
